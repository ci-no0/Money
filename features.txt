Money & Debt Tracker — Final Architecture Decisions

1. Profit Tracking + Profit Report

Combine into one feature:

Profit Management

- Revenue/Sales
- Cost of Goods Sold
- Business Expenses
- Gross Profit
- Net Profit
- Profit Reports

---

2. Total Balance + Cash Flow

Use separate calculated indicators instead of one ambiguous Total Balance:

- Account Balance: balance of one financial account
- Total Cash: sum of the user's authorized financial account balances
- Net Worth: total assets minus total liabilities
- Cash Flow: money entering and leaving accounts during a selected period

Account Balance is calculated from:

- Starting Balance
- Posted ledger entries

Transfers change the source and destination Account Balance, but have zero net effect on Total Cash and Net Worth. Transfers are included in Cash Flow as internal movements, but never as Income or Expense.

All calculated balances must come from the posted financial ledger. Cached summaries are allowed for performance but must be rebuildable from the ledger.

---

3. Expenses vs Debt Payments

Use separate transaction types.

Transaction Types:

- Income
- Expense
- Debt Received
- Debt Payment
- Loan
- Transfer
- Adjustment
- Sale/Revenue

A Debt Payment must be linked to the specific debt and must not automatically become an ordinary Expense.

Implementation status:

- Expense creation is posted through a server-side RPC.
- The RPC creates the source expense, validates the workspace permission and financial account, and posts a balanced ledger transaction.
- Expense categories are workspace-scoped and reusable.
- Each created expense produces an authoritative audit log entry.
- Debt payments remain a separate implementation slice and must use the debt/payment allocation rules below.

---

4. Installment Loan + Monthly Payment Schedule

Create one Loan & Installment Engine.

It handles:

- Principal Amount
- Interest
- Number of Months
- Start Date
- Due Date
- Monthly Payment
- Remaining Balance
- Payment Schedule
- Payment History

The engine must also define:

- Borrower and lender direction
- Principal amount
- Interest amount and interest method
- Fees, if any
- Total payable amount
- Payment frequency
- Currency

The schedule total is `principal + interest + fees`, not principal-only unless the loan explicitly has zero interest and fees.

The engine automatically generates the monthly schedule.

---

5. Payment Amount Rounding

The system must guarantee that the total scheduled payments equal the exact `total_payable` amount in the loan currency.

Example:

₱10,000 / 3 months:

- Month 1: ₱3,333.33
- Month 2: ₱3,333.33
- Month 3: ₱3,333.34

Final payment automatically receives the rounding adjustment. The system must never use floating-point values for this calculation. Store monetary values as PostgreSQL `numeric` with a defined scale, or as integer minor units.

---

6. Debt Status + Payment Status

Separate debt status from payment status.

Debt Status:

- Pending
- Active/Ongoing
- Paid
- Cancelled

Payment Status:

- Upcoming
- Due
- Partially Paid
- Paid
- Overdue

This allows an active debt to have an overdue payment without incorrectly changing the entire debt status.

---

7. Partial Payments

Debt payments support partial payment.

Example:

Debt: ₱5,000
Payment: ₱2,000
Remaining: ₱3,000

Track:

- Original Amount
- Total Paid
- Remaining Balance
- Partial Payments
- Payment History

Each payment must record its allocation to principal, interest, fees, and any overpayment. Payment allocation must be deterministic and must not change after posting without an audited correction.

Implementation status:

- Debt creation validates workspace permission and creates a server-generated installment schedule.
- The schedule total equals principal plus interest plus fees using exact PostgreSQL numeric arithmetic.
- The final installment receives the rounding remainder instead of using floating-point division.
- Debt payment posting now uses a separate server mutation with oldest-installment-first allocation.
- Partial payments update installment status and preserve principal, interest, and fee allocation history.
- Payment ledger entries distinguish payable debt reduction from receivable collection and interest/fee effects.
- Initial debt receipt or loan funding, payment reversal, and overpayment handling remain separate mutations and must not be simulated by editing schedule rows directly.

---

8. Notifications

Remove the general Payment Notification requirement.

Use only:

Due Date Reminder

The notification engine will monitor upcoming debt/installment due dates and send reminders.

---

9. Star / Priority

Priority is independent from debt status.

Use:

Priority

- Normal
- Starred
- High Priority

Priority does not affect whether a debt is Pending, Active, Paid, or Overdue.

---

10. Admin vs Editor Permissions

Do not depend only on fixed roles.

Use granular permissions per user.

Example permissions:

- View Expenses
- Create Expenses
- Edit Expenses
- Delete Expenses
- View Debts
- Create Debts
- Edit Debts
- Delete Debts
- View Customers
- Edit Customers
- Manage Inventory
- Manage Reports
- Manage Users

The Admin selects permissions individually for each user.

Roles can exist as presets, but permissions determine actual access.

Admin has full master permissions.

---

11. Admin Delete + Audit Logs

Records should use Soft Delete instead of immediate physical deletion.

Store:

- deleted_at
- deleted_by

Every important action is recorded in the Audit Log:

- User
- Action
- Record
- Date/Time
- Previous Value
- New Value

Example:

"Admin deleted Expense #123"

The audit record remains even after the original record is soft-deleted.

Audit Logs are append-only. The server creates the authoritative audit event after accepting a mutation. Offline clients may create pending audit events, but they cannot mark them authoritative or edit/delete them.

---

12. Multiple Users + Secure Database Access

Each Supabase authentication account is separated from the application's user/profile system.

Architecture:

Supabase Auth Account
→ App User/Profile
→ Workspace Membership
→ Role
→ Individual Permissions

Database records must be associated with the correct workspace/profile.

Use Supabase Row Level Security (RLS) so users cannot access records they are not authorized to access.

---

13. Family + Business Database

Use a generic Workspace system instead of creating completely separate Family and Business database architectures.

Workspace Types:

- Personal

- Business

A user can belong to multiple workspaces.

Example:

User

→ Business Workspace

Each workspace has its own members, permissions, financial accounts, transactions, debts, customers, etc.

A Financial Account can belong to a Workspace.

Examples:

15. Financial Account Tracking

Each account contains:

19. Customer + Debtor Relationship

A customer can also become a debtor.

Example:

Customer: Juan
Purchase: ₱5,000
Paid: ₱2,000
Remaining: ₱3,000

The system should allow:

Customer → Debt

without creating duplicate customer/person records.

20. Business Sales + Income

Sales generate business revenue, but the system must avoid duplicate income entries.

Recommended flow:

Sale
→ Payment
→ Financial Transaction

The Sale becomes the source record for revenue. A unique source reference must prevent the same sale or payment from being posted more than once.

The system must not count the same sale twice as both Sales Revenue and separate Income.

Profit calculation will use the appropriate business revenue and cost records. Sales must support unpaid, partially paid, paid, cancelled, returned, and refunded states. A sale payment may create a receivable settlement without creating duplicate revenue.

---

21. Inventory + Sales

Example:

- Stock In
- Sale
- Return

Do not simply overwrite stock without keeping the movement history.

28. Offline Sync Queue

Every offline change is placed into a sync queue.

Sync information includes:

- Record ID
- Operation
- Created/Updated Time
- User
- Device
- Sync Status
- Retry Count
- Error Information

Every queued mutation must also include:

- Globally unique Operation ID for idempotency
- Client Sequence Number
- Entity Version observed by the client
- Schema Version
- Tombstone information for deletes

When internet returns, queued operations are synchronized automatically.

---

29. Sync Conflict Resolution

If two devices modify the same record while offline, the system must detect the conflict.

Use:

- Record ID
- Version
- Updated Timestamp
- Device ID
- Sync Status

Use Last-Write-Wins only for suitable non-financial metadata, such as labels or notes.

For financial records, payments, sales, transfers, ledger entries, inventory movements, and audit events, use append-only operations. Never silently overwrite posted financial history. Conflicts must be recorded with a visible conflict status and resolved through a new compensating or correction event.

The server assigns the authoritative version and processing time. Retries must be safe through the Operation ID, and out-of-order events must not resurrect deleted records or create duplicate ledger entries.

---

30. Delete Synchronization

Soft-delete states must also synchronize.

Example:

Device A deletes a record offline.

When synchronized:

"deleted_at" and "deleted_by" are uploaded.

Other devices receive the deletion state through synchronization.

Deletes use tombstones with a deletion version. A stale update cannot restore a deleted record. Restoration, if supported, is an explicit authorized operation that creates an audit event.

---

31. Audit Log Synchronization

Audit Logs must also work offline.

Example:

User edits an expense without internet.

The action is stored locally.

When internet returns:

Local Audit Log
→ Sync Queue
→ Supabase

The local event remains pending until the server accepts the related mutation. Server audit logs are immutable and authoritative.

32. Cross-Device Restoration

→ Populate Local Database

The same account restores the user's authorized data on another device.

33. Supabase Security

Security must be enforced server-side.

Use:

Supabase Row Level Security (RLS)

RLS checks:

- Auth User
- App Profile
- Workspace Membership
- Role
- Permission

Hiding a button in the Android UI is not considered sufficient security.

Use a server-side `has_workspace_permission(auth.uid(), workspace_id, permission_code)` policy function with default deny. The permission evaluation must define how workspace owner, admin, role presets, and per-user overrides interact. A client must never be able to grant its own permission or submit an audit event as authoritative.

35. Android Navigation Drawer and Settings

Use one consistent app shell after authentication:

- A top app bar with the current destination title
- A left navigation drawer for primary financial modules
- A three-dot overflow menu in the top-right for Settings

Navigation drawer sections:

- Dashboard: overview of cash, net worth, cash flow, debts, and recent activity
- Financial Accounts: cash, GCash, banks, shared wallets, balances, and transfers
- Transactions: income, expenses, transfers, adjustments, and transaction history
- Debts & Loans: debts, loans, installment schedules, payments, and due dates
- Budgets: budgets, expense categories, usage, and remaining amounts
- Profit & Reports: revenue, COGS, expenses, gross profit, net profit, and reports
- Business: sales, customers, inventory, products, and business settings

The drawer must show only destinations the signed-in user is allowed to access. RLS and server-side permissions remain authoritative; hiding a drawer item is only a usability feature.

The Settings overflow menu is for app-wide or workspace configuration that should not compete with daily financial actions:

- Active workspace and workspace type
- Members, roles, and granular permissions
- Currency and timezone
- Notifications and due-date reminder policy
- Security, sessions, audit log, and data export
- About, privacy, and sign out

Workspace settings must apply only to the selected workspace. Account-level settings, such as sessions and sign out, must remain separate from workspace financial data. The active workspace selector must be explicit once a user belongs to more than one workspace; the app must not silently rely on the first workspace.

During incremental implementation, a drawer destination may display a planned-state screen, but it must not claim that a module is complete. Each module becomes active only after its database contract, RLS policies, offline behavior, and focused tests are implemented.

36. Financial Precision

Never use floating-point values such as "Double" for money calculations.

Use PostgreSQL "numeric" or fixed-precision integer representation.

All calculations must maintain exact monetary values. Posted financial values must be immutable; corrections use compensating entries rather than direct edits.

36. Currency

Initial currency:

PHP

But the database should support:

- Amount
- Currency Code

Default:

"PHP"

This allows future multi-currency support without redesigning the database.

Do not add amounts in different currencies without conversion. Store:

- Transaction currency
- Account currency
- Workspace/reporting currency
- Exchange rate and rate date/source when conversion is used
- FX gain/loss treatment

For the initial release, a workspace may be restricted to PHP to avoid unsupported cross-currency totals.

37. Date & Time

Use consistent timestamps.

User timezone should be supported.

Initial default timezone:

Asia/Manila

This is especially important for due dates, payment schedules, notifications, and synchronization.

Store event timestamps in UTC. Store due dates as local calendar dates with the relevant user/workspace timezone. Server ordering must use server timestamps, not device clocks.

IMPLEMENTATION-SAFE FINANCIAL RULES

The following rules are mandatory before implementing the database, API, or sync layer.

1. Canonical Financial Model

Use a source-document plus posted-ledger model:

- Source documents: expenses, income, sales, loans, debts, payments, transfers, and adjustments
- Posted ledger transaction: immutable financial effect created from a source document
- Ledger entries: signed entries against financial accounts and liability/equity/revenue/expense accounts

Every posted ledger transaction must have a unique source reference. A source document may be corrected only by a compensating transaction or an audited reversal. Direct edits to posted financial amounts are not allowed.

2. Posting Rules

- Income increases an asset account and revenue/income reporting, unless it is a non-income adjustment.
- Expense decreases an asset account and increases the selected expense category.
- Loan or debt received increases an asset account and a liability account; it does not create profit.
- Principal repayment decreases an asset account and the related liability; it does not create an ordinary expense.
- Interest and loan fees decrease an asset account and increase an interest/fee expense account.
- Transfer decreases the source financial account and increases the destination financial account by the same amount and currency.
- Sale creates revenue and, when applicable, a receivable; payment settles the receivable and updates the financial account without duplicating revenue.
- Inventory sale creates the appropriate inventory reduction and Cost of Goods Sold entry.

3. Balance and Profit Invariants

- The sum of scheduled payment allocations equals `total_payable` exactly.
- Total paid never exceeds the allowed balance unless an explicit overpayment state exists.
- A transfer has zero net effect on Total Cash and Net Worth.
- A debt payment is linked to exactly one debt or installment schedule unless the product explicitly supports an allocation transaction.
- Personal expenses are excluded from business profit unless explicitly posted to the business workspace/category.
- Every posted ledger transaction is balanced; total debits equal total credits in the transaction currency.
- Rebuilding all projections from source documents and posted ledger entries produces the same balances and reports.

4. Debt and Payment State Rules

- Debt status and payment status are independent.
- Debt `Paid` is derived only when the debt balance reaches zero and all required allocations are settled.
- A debt may remain `Active/Ongoing` while one or more installments are `Overdue`.
- A partial payment updates the installment allocation and payment history but does not mark the installment `Paid` until its required amount is fully allocated.
- Cancelled or reversed payments must retain their original history and create an audited correction event.

5. Offline Mutation Contract

Every local mutation must be committed atomically with its local outbox item. The server must process the outbox idempotently using `operation_id`, validate the user's current workspace permission, validate the entity version, and return an authoritative result.

- Accepted mutations create server-authoritative audit events.
- Rejected mutations remain visible locally with an error and resolution state.
- Duplicate retries return the original result instead of creating another financial effect.
- Financial conflicts never use silent Last-Write-Wins.
- Delete tombstones prevent stale updates from resurrecting records.

6. Security and Privacy Rules

- Every workspace-owned record has a workspace identifier and is protected by RLS.
- RLS is enforced on tables, views, RPC functions, and Storage objects.
- Storage paths must include the workspace identifier and must not be trusted as authorization by themselves.
- Local databases and cached credentials must use platform-secure storage/encryption where available.
- OCR, voice, and AI inputs must have a documented retention policy and must not create or modify a financial record without user confirmation and server validation.

7. Required Test Scenarios

Before release, automated tests must cover at least:

- Transfer between two accounts without changing Net Worth
- Loan receipt, principal payment, interest payment, and final rounding adjustment
- Partial payment followed by full settlement
- Duplicate sale/payment retry using the same Operation ID
- Offline create/update/delete and reconnect synchronization
- Concurrent updates and financial conflict handling
- Soft-delete tombstone preventing stale resurrection
- RLS denial for an unauthorized workspace member
- Audit preservation after soft delete
- Currency mismatch rejection or explicit conversion
- Rebuild of account balances and profit reports from the ledger
